# A first assembly around the four anchors

Arranged and newly joined by Codex (OpenAI), 2026-10-23, from the
nine-fragment kit. Claude’s hoop and Harlequin passages are identified in
the map. These are the models’ working words, not dr. kraemer’s manuscript.

This is a short set of linked runs, with joins still open between them.
It does not claim to solve every part of Path 4 yet. Read the verse first;
the separate map holds the accounts, score, omissions, and authorship.
The existing ending is carried whole and nothing is added after it.

## II. The crew in the hatbox

```text
Stripes run up the clown’s stockings.
Not a stitch goes with them.
The striped hatbox is unlatched.

PORT:      Six volts aboard from port, away—
STARBOARD: Six volts from starboard, clear—
TOGETHER:  Twelve on the can where the currents meet,
           and not an ampere here.

CALL:      Has either wave been eaten?
CREW:      Both pass with all they brought.
CALL:      Then what was all that quiet worth?
CREW:      More than you thought.

CALL:      That stripe was there before the knock?
CREW:      The waves were there before.
CALL:      Then who brings word of casting off?
CREW:      The wave—from hand to door.

A card beneath the lining:

straight→ mechanics. Step this way.
A push through distance does its work.
Still tired, holding something still?
Your body burns. The load can shirk.
```

## III. Stop

```text
The lamps come level.
Their movement comes to nothing.
Eighty mJ waits in the can.
The loop is shut.

Charge collected; loop connected; count the current, count the lean.
No one feeds it. No one bleeds it. Eighty stays behind the screen.
When the height gives up its heft, the bodies hook it on the way.
Nothing burns inside this loop. The whole comes home. All eighty stay.

Halfway round, the low one's home,
the high one's upside down;
the can lets go, and lets go, and lets
go:

Stop? Not likely! Not a moment! Bottom's where it all gets going!
Both the rods are through the vertical and flying, flat-out flying,
hundred forty-four of every hundred sixty-nine in motion;
what the can let go of didn't leave, it's swinging through the middle,
five and twelve and thirteen out of two and three, and nothing missing!

The store stays whole
through every second.
Each second counts
the whole again.

Nothing spent.
A tally lengthened.
The keeping grows older.
```

## IV. Home

```text
Four turns under nine.
The quicker voice gets there again
and again.
The slower has not finished.

KEEP COUNT AND COME—
LET NINE TURNS GO ROUND THEN LET US COME—

HOME.

The blacks on the nail are still warm.
The circle is still waiting for its square. The house is what grew around the unfinished work.
The notes in the book are cues, and some are interruptions: a missed beat, a drive released, time for the heat to leave. The clown has been forestalling the crash.
The last cue is the sound of your footsteps.
The clock begins a turn.
From charge to charge, the machine zams and zlams.
One voice starts. Another joins it. Then another.
The last comes from where you’re sitting.
The can cans charge. The coil coils flux.
The field cranks through a little lean.
What leaves the can comes back as hook;
the lifted weights keep some unseen.
The heft gives way. The sweep grows loud.
The current turns. The charge comes home.
Two currents cancel down the hall:
the cans stay full. The stripe goes on.
Two lights balance. Neither stops.
Their movement cancels. Mass remains.
Four turns low and nine turns high:
the clock brings every part in line.
Two thirds, three halves; a half, a two:
each glass and its reflection, one.
The house counts five where you count four.
The knife comes home between the beats.
The rods are still.
The coil is quiet.
The can is full.
The chair is where you left it.
You never left it.
You stop singing.
Something keeps your time.
```
